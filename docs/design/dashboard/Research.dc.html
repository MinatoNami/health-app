What the category does, and what was taken from it

Whoop, Oura, and the quantified-self aggregators Exist.io and Gyroscope. Four principles are worth copying, one is worth refusing, and the most useful finding was not about design at all.

Taken — one big thing
Oura's redesign “cuts through the clutter to help you focus on one big thing.” Whoop's top tier carries scores and no graphs at all. This dashboard led with Records in range — 47,213, which is a property of the database. The lead is a sentence about the person now, and the row count moves to where plumbing questions are asked.
Taken — three tiers, three screens
Whoop separates glanceable, trend, and deep-dive onto their own screens rather than collapsing sections on one page — “clean mental boundaries.” Overview keeps two charts and hands the rest to Explore, which already exists and was being bypassed. Three tiers, three screens: glance, ask, explore. The analysis is not a fourth — it opens Insights, where asking about it is already the next click.
Taken — statements, not just charts
The review that separates Exist.io from the prettier Gyroscope: “Exist allows you to make something of it.” Its signature output is a sentence — you are moderately less likely to be productive when you walk more. This server already writes exactly that shape and nothing displayed it.
Taken — type carries the hierarchy
Whoop sets its score at roughly 72pt for arm's-length reading and keeps everything else deliberately small. Here the lead sentence takes that weight instead of a number, and the tiles drop to a supporting rank rather than competing with it.
Refused — the composite score
Whoop's Recovery and Oura's Readiness are the category's defining feature: a 0–100 number blending HRV, resting heart rate, temperature, sleep and prior activity. It is also the one thing this product has already decided it will not ship — no invented composite score, because the weighting would be a judgement nobody measured. Drawn as a sketch on the dark board so the choice is visible rather than silently skipped.
The finding that mattered

Most of what the popular apps do that this one does not is already computed by this server and never requested by the browser. Six endpoints, all tested, all returning a written sentence with their numbers — and the dashboard calls none of them. The first three fill Insights before a question is asked — put where the reader's next move already is, rather than behind a tab of their own.

Endpoint What it already returns Where it now appears
analysis/correlations Driver vs outcome, both group means, lag, a written description Insights — on open
analysis/patterns Day-group means and a significance word against your own variation Insights — on open
analysis/anomalies Metrics sitting away from baseline, with direction Insights — on open
health/coverage Valid days out of window days, per metric Overview lead, tiles, Insights rail
insights/daily The deterministic morning brief the phone already shows Overview lead sentence
analysis/trend Fitted direction over a window — still unused, worth a look next
What was kept
The palette, unchanged. It was validated for lightness band, chroma floor, colour-vision separation and contrast in both modes, and it still passes — four distinguishable chart series, a real light mode, and status colours split into mark and text variants because the amber that works as a bar is unreadable as type. The phone's fixed dark palette solves none of those and would have cost all of them.
What makes them one product
Structure, not colour: the monospaced uppercase section label, the seven-segment coverage strip, hairline cards, one tinted delta per row, and the rule that a gap is drawn as a gap rather than joined across. All of it lifted from the phone's boards, none of it requiring the dashboard to give up light mode.
