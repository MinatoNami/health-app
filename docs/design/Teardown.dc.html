Teardown — what is on screen today
The app follows Apple Health so closely it inherits Health's problem
MetricStyle.swift says the colour language copies Health "so there is no second colour language to learn". That is a sound call for colour and the wrong call for the home screen: Health is a database browser, and this app is the one thing Health is not — something that has already done the thinking.
Summary, as shipped
Six numbers, all shouting equally
Steps at +32% and Weight at −2% get identical weight, type size and colour. The screen has no opinion, so the reader has to form one. Nothing here says this is the thing that changed.
The best asset is invisible
The server computes a confidence grade and a reason for every figure — "7 of 7 days have data". None of it reaches this screen. The one thing no competitor ships is the one thing not shown.
Three of five tabs are plumbing
Metrics, Server and Settings are all "is the machine working". Real estate at the bottom of the screen is the most expensive in the app, and 60% of it is diagnostics.
A permission card that never leaves
"Grant Health access to begin" occupies the top third above the actual content. Onboarding state is sitting in the permanent layout.
Worth keeping: the restraint
No gamification, no streaks, no invented score, no badges. The docstring on StatusView — "healthy days say almost nothing, which is what makes the bad day visible" — is a better design principle than most shipped health apps have.
Worth keeping: personal baselines
Every figure is already "against your own preceding 28 days", never a population norm. Whoop charges a subscription for that idea. It is stated here in 8pt grey.
